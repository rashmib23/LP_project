3. CONTENT OUTLINE
------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Bias-Variance Tradeoff (20 min)
    • Bias: error from wrong assumptions in the model; leads to underfitting.
    • Variance: error from sensitivity to small fluctuations in training data;
      leads to overfitting.
    • Total expected error = Bias² + Variance + Irreducible noise.
    • Visualization: training error vs. test error as model complexity increases
      (U-shaped test error curve).
    • Practical examples: linear model on non-linear data (high bias);
      high-degree polynomial on small dataset (high variance).

3.2 Regularization (25 min)
    • Motivation: constrain model complexity to reduce variance without
      dramatically increasing bias.

    L2 Ridge Regression:
      J(w) = MSE(w) + λ Σ wᵢ²
      Effect: shrinks all coefficients toward zero; no coefficient becomes
      exactly zero; closed-form solution exists.

    L1 Lasso Regression:
      J(w) = MSE(w) + λ Σ |wᵢ|
      Effect: can drive some coefficients to exactly zero → automatic
      feature selection; no closed-form; solved via coordinate descent.

    Elastic Net (brief mention): combines L1 and L2 penalties.
    Choosing λ: small λ → complex model; large λ → simple model.

3.3 Cross-Validation (20 min)
    • Holdout validation: simple but high variance due to single split.
    • k-Fold CV: dataset split into k folds; model trained k times, each
      time using a different fold as validation set. Final score = mean
      of k validation scores.
    • Stratified k-Fold: preserves class distribution in each fold.
    • GridSearchCV in scikit-learn: exhaustive search over a parameter
      grid using CV to find optimal hyperparameters.

3.4 Hands-on Activity (20 min)
    • Students load the California Housing dataset.
    • Fit Ridge, Lasso, and plain LinearRegression for λ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}.
    • Plot training vs. validation RMSE against log(λ).
    • Identify the λ that minimises test RMSE and explain the trend in
      non-zero coefficients for Lasso.

3.5 Wrap-up and Q&A (5 min)
    • Quick oral recap: what happens to bias and variance as λ increases?
    • Preview of next class: Support Vector Machines (Unit IV).

4. TEACHING METHODS AND STRATEGIES
------------------------------------------------------------

  Method 1 – Lecture with Whiteboard Demonstration (35 min)
    The instructor will deliver a structured lecture using slides for the
    theoretical portion. The bias-variance tradeoff will be drawn live on
    the whiteboard to emphasize the U-shaped error curve. Mathematical
    derivations of L1 and L2 penalties will be written step-by-step so
    students can follow the algebra.

  Method 2 – Demonstration / Live Coding (20 min)
    The instructor will demonstrate a live Python notebook (projected on
    screen) showing how Ridge and Lasso affect coefficient magnitudes.
    Students follow along on their laptops or observe the screen.

  Method 3 – Activity-Based / Hands-on Lab (20 min)
    Students open the pre-distributed Jupyter notebook
    (lab06_regularization.ipynb). In pairs, they complete the code cells,
    interpret the plots, and answer three guided questions.

  Method 4 – Discussion (10 min)
    A whole-class think-pair-share discussion: "Which regularizer would
    you choose if you suspect only 5 out of 20 features are relevant?
    Justify your answer with reference to today's content."

  Method 5 – Inquiry-based Reflection (5 min)
    Students individually answer a one-minute paper: "What is one question
    you still have about regularization?" Questions are collected and will
    be addressed at the start of the next class.

8. ANTICIPATED STUDENT DIFFICULTIES AND MITIGATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
  Difficulty 1: Confusion between bias and variance in practical terms.
  Mitigation  : Use the dartboard analogy (clustered but off-centre = high
                bias; spread out = high variance) before the formal definition.

  Difficulty 2: Understanding why L1 produces sparse solutions.
  Mitigation  : Draw the diamond constraint region and show geometrically
                why the optimum often lands at a corner (coefficient = 0).

  Difficulty 3: Interpreting GridSearchCV output.
  Mitigation  : Walk through cv_results_ dictionary in the live demo;
                highlight best_params_ and best_score_.
